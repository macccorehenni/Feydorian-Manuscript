<h1>FERRIS
</h1><div>We walked forward, past the towering life tree, its branches shifting in the breeze like a silent witness to something inevitable. The way it stood—rigid, expectant—it almost seemed to anticipate what came next, dreading it in some unspoken way. But I didn’t share its grief. I felt something else entirely.
</div><div>Relief.
</div><div>The Lich was still out there, still hunting, still pushing his shadow over Feydor, but the weight of him wasn’t on my shoulders right now. Not here. Not with Yae at my side, the road stretching open before us.
</div><div>"Yae?"
</div><div>"Mm?"
</div><div>Her response was soft, absent-minded, but I saw the flicker of attention in her gaze when she looked at me.
</div><div>"Do you feel it? Like... a weight’s been lifted?"
</div><div>She let out a slow breath, something between an exhale and a laugh. "Yeah. Feels easier out here."
</div><div>She wouldn’t elaborate. She didn’t need to. I had seen the way she carried herself these past months—tense, cautious, almost waiting for the moment she had to run again. But now? Now she was walking, moving forward, choosing her direction.
</div><div>Something caught my eye—a strange bloom nestled between the roots ahead.
</div><div>"What is this plant?"
</div><div>Yae crouched beside it, studying its petals.
</div><div>"Feybloom?"
</div><div>"That’s what I thought, but..."
</div><div>She frowned. "Doesn’t feel right. It’s off—ugh, I kinda wanna pull it."
</div><div>Her fingers closed around the stem.
</div><div>The moment she touched it, she tensed. A sharp inhale, a slight recoil—she dropped it as if it had burned her.
</div><div>"Okay, that’s... weird. Not warm like Feydorian. Not minty like High Forest."
</div><div>She picked it up again, slower this time, tucking it beside her other Feybloom.
</div><div>"I’ll check with someone who might know."
</div><div>"Yae?"
</div><div>She met my gaze, and for the first time in weeks, the tension between my shoulders eased.
</div><div>"You good?"
</div><div>She hesitated, then nodded. "Yeah, I’m fine."
</div><div>And I believed her.
</div><div>
</div><h1>YAE
</h1><div>Finally—back on the road again.
</div><div>The past weeks had been restless, difficult, but the difference was tangible now. The weight that had pressed against my ribs, the ache of uncertainty, had lifted—if only slightly. The path ahead was still uncertain, but I was moving, and that was enough.
</div><div>Still, something lingered.
</div><div>Ferris.
</div><div>Was he in love with me? The thought had surfaced, stubborn and inexplicable. I had pushed it aside more than once, dismissed it entirely. It wasn’t possible. It wasn’t real. But something about the way he watched me unsettled my certainty.
</div><div>And then there was my father—Danche.
</div><div>I could feel him here. Not physically, not in any tangible way, but in the air, in the hum of the leaves, in the quiet pulse of the ground beneath my feet.
</div><div>Whether imagined or real, it didn’t matter. He was cheering this on.
</div><div>Still, misgivings lingered. Not just about Ferris, but about my father, about the tangled ties between us all. I couldn’t begin to unravel them, but I could focus on something simpler—something concrete.
</div><div>The plant.
</div><div>It wasn’t Feybloom.
</div><div>It was something else. Something that hummed against my skin in a way Feybloom never had.
</div><div>The contact I had mentioned was a treant, someone I had met after fleeing Secomber—after that merchant had vanished like he had never existed in the first place. A skilled herbologist, infinitely more knowledgeable than me. He had been kindly, patient, wise. If anyone could trace the bloom’s origins, it was him.
</div><div>But that was later.
</div><div>Now, we walked.
</div><div>Ferris kept pace with me, despite being two decades older than I was. If anything, he seemed stronger than me. I kept stopping, out of breath, but when I did, he stopped too.
</div><div>Twilight came fast, shadows stretching long against the ground.
</div><div>And then—
</div><div>I felt it.
</div><div>Something in the air.
</div><div>The scent of damp earth thickened. The whisper of the leaves turned restless. The Hollow Grove, just west of the mountains, had always felt strange beneath my feet, but now it was different—tainted.
</div><div>I barely had time to warn Ferris before the first shape lunged forward.
</div><div>It wasn’t alive, but it moved as though it had once been. Limbs of bark and bone snapped unnaturally, jerking toward me with eerie precision. My blade was already in my hand.
</div><div>Instinct took over.
</div><div>Steel met unnatural flesh, cutting through its gnarled body—but the thing didn’t stop. The wound splintered, twisted, reformed as if bound by some unseen force.
</div><div>Ferris hesitated.
</div><div>I saw it.
</div><div>That moment of uncertainty, sharp and audible in the way his breath caught.
</div><div>"Ferris! Eyes forward—move!"
</div><div>He snapped into motion. His stance steadied, and he joined me.
</div><div>The battle became rhythm—cut, move, strike, defend. These creatures were not human, not beast. They were something in-between, something corrupted. They moved with unnatural precision, shifting against the wind like whispers given form.
</div><div>One collapsed into dust beneath my strike. Then another.
</div><div>The final creature lunged—Ferris struck first, his blade catching its side. It shuddered, limbs locking, then crumbled at his feet.
</div><div>Silence settled.
</div><div>The forest still whispered. The ground still felt wrong.
</div><div>But for now, the battle was done.
</div><div>"Ferris, you good?"
</div><div>His breath was ragged, but something sparked in his voice—exhilaration, maybe.
</div><div>"I—yeah. That felt... strangely good."
</div><div>"Are you hurt, my…" I hesitated, then, with rare caution, added, "friend?"
</div><div>It felt different, but was it more than friendship?
</div><div>I wasn’t sure.
</div><div>The creeping suspicion remained.
</div><div>"No, no, I'm good, just out of breath," he said, shaking his head.
</div><div>"This might be a good time to camp," I suggested, "before we head back into the forest."
</div><div>"That isn't a bad idea."
</div><div>I started gathering sticks, tree branches, weaving together the structure of the nest I’d built countless times before.
</div><div>"Ferris! Help me—grab some straw or needles or something."
</div><div>He complied, gathering needles from the enchanted oak tree I had started my platform upon. With time, and with ruthless efficiency, we completed our shelter.
</div><div>Jumping up without hesitation, I all but dragged Ferris up with me.
</div><div>"My—when you said your nest was high, I didn’t imagine that high!" he huffed between breaths.
</div><div>"You’ll get used to it," I said, setting my bag down, making a makeshift shelf like I’d done a thousand times before. My bedroll came next, unstrapped from behind Verdant Leaf, set neatly in place.
</div><div>Ferris hesitated before following suit.
</div><div>"You know, Yae… I think he…" Ferris gestured vaguely—he meant the Lich. "He’s still hunting you."
</div><div>"But you know what, Ferris?" I replied, my voice carrying something sharp—something sure. "We’re ready for him."
</div><div>I turned to him, expecting something—some sort of response.
</div><div>But he was already asleep.
</div><div>I let out a breath, stood, and wandered to the edge of the platform, looking out at the stars of the High Forest.
</div><div>We were ready.
