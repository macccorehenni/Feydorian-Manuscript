## Description & History


<div>Queen Amenle, weakened by years of exile and burdened by the truth she had carried in silence, lay upon her deathbed. The years of fleeing Mayldor had worn her down, stripping away the strength she once carried with effortless grace. Yet in this final moment, as twilight crept along the edges of the sky, there was no more reason to keep secrets.
</div><div>She reached for Yaereene’s hand, her grip frail but determined, her breath shallow yet steady enough to speak.
</div><div>This was the moment of revelation—the truth of Yae’s birth, long hidden, now given freely.
</div><div>She spoke of the vision that had come before that day, of the presence of Danche, of his voice weaving prophecy into the air like something inevitable. She recalled the weight upon her chest, the sickness that overcame her, the healer’s words that had forced her to confront what she had long refused to see.
</div><div>And then, she spoke of Yae herself.
</div><div>Of the moment she looked down and saw her, ready, already belonging to the world before she had even understood.
</div><div>She had not chosen this.
</div><div>She had not prepared for it.
</div><div>But it had come all the same.
</div><div>And now, with the last of her strength, she let the truth slip free.
</div><div>This was the prophecy fulfilled.
</div><div>This was the weight lifted.
</div><div>This was her final gift
.</div><div><hr></div><h1>Amalene</h1><div>I had a vision the day before you were born, Yaereene. It was not the first, but it was the only one that left me shaken.

</div><div>It came while I was praying at the tree, kneeling beneath its branches as I had done countless times before. The wind carried no warning—only the shift of the leaves, the sound of the forest breathing. And then, he was there.

</div><div>Your true father.

</div><div>Danche stood before me, his form that of a great stag, silver-antlered, his gaze deep as the roots beneath us. He did not command—he did not need to. His presence alone was enough.

</div><div>I did not move.

</div><div>"Your Majesty," he said, his voice layered, ancient, quiet but undeniable. "You are meant for more than tending to my tree. There is more that waits for you."

</div><div>I did not speak.

</div><div>"You have served well. You have steadied the king’s hand when it wavered. You have shouldered burdens not meant to be yours, endured absence and uncertainty in the name of duty. And still, you remained open, still, you welcomed what came—both the man and the war he carried."

</div><div>His words carried no judgment. Only truth.

</div><div>"But soon, that will change."

</div><div>I felt the weight of it before he spoke it.

</div><div>"Feydor will not remain at peace. One enemy, with charisma worth a thousand words, will split your kingdom in half. You will run. You will have no recourse but to flee."

</div><div>A prophecy spoken not as warning, but as certainty.

</div><div>"But you, Amenle of Mayldor, are meant for greater things. Not by your hand—another’s."

</div><div>I barely breathed. The forest stood still around us, as if listening.

</div><div>"I present you with the gift of life, to fulfill the prophecy I have set forth."

</div><div>The air shifted.

</div><div>"Goodbye, my lady."

</div><div>And then, he was gone.

</div><div>I have held onto this secret for so long, Yaereene. Too long.
</div><div>I felt the weight of it before I knew its name—a pressure in my chest, a quiet hum beneath my ribs that refused to fade. I ignored it. I buried it beneath duty, beneath reason, convincing myself that it was nothing.
</div><div>But I should have known better.
</div><div>When Danche appeared before me—when he spoke of fate, of a future already set—I refused to understand. I told myself it was prophecy, something distant, something I could push away. I did not want to believe that he had already shaped my path long before I had a chance to walk it.
</div><div>And yet, the truth was always waiting.
</div><div>The next day, when the sickness took me, I went to the healer. I still believed it was nothing, just exhaustion, just another burden to carry. But when she looked at me—when she said the words—everything I had buried came crashing forward.
</div><div>"You are in labor."
</div><div>I laughed. Not because I didn’t believe her, but because I couldn’t believe her. Because the moment I did, the weight I had ignored would become real.
</div><div>Then I looked down.
</div><div>And I saw you.
</div><div>You were already there, waiting—already part of this world before I had even prepared myself to hold you. And I broke.
</div><div>Because Danche had been right.
</div><div>Because I had known, deep down, all along.
</div><div>And so I wept.
</div><div>For the truth I had refused to see.
</div><div>For the fate that had already been written.
</div><div>For you.
</div><div>And now, I tell you, because I can no longer hold it.
</div><div>You were never a choice. You were never a possibility. You were always meant to be.
</div><div>And nothing—not time, not denial, not even my fear—could have changed that.
